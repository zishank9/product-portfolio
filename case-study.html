Improving Enterprise Data Migration and Validation

How I worked with an enterprise client and engineering teams to translate legacy data migration requirements into an ETL solution, with a focus on data integrity, financial consistency, and reporting accuracy.

Role: Product Delivery Manager / Business Analysis and Delivery

← Back to portfolio

1. The problem

An enterprise hospitality client needed to migrate historical hotel and guest data from a legacy Property Management System (PMS) into HotelKey's platform.

The source data was supplied in large, compressed XML files. The information needed to be understood, mapped to the target system's data model, transformed, and loaded into the new platform.

Successful migration also required more than transferring records. Financial balances needed to remain consistent, and reports needed to reflect the migrated data correctly.

2. The objective

Help establish a repeatable ETL process that could migrate the required client data while maintaining data integrity and improving confidence in financial and reporting results.

  • Understand the client's migration requirements.
  • Map source data fields to the target data structure.
  • Enable the engineering team to build a suitable ETL tool.
  • Validate migrated data against expected results.
  • Identify discrepancies affecting financial balances and reports.

3. My role and contributions

  • Requirement gathering: Worked directly with the client to understand which historical data needed to be transferred.
  • Data mapping: Analysed sample source files and created field mappings to translate the required information into the target data structure.
  • User stories: Converted migration requirements into user stories for the development team.
  • Engineering collaboration: Worked with developers as they built an ETL tool that accepted client files, transformed the data, and loaded it into the target database.
  • Data validation: Used sample data and validation exercises to check whether the migrated output matched expectations.
  • Reporting reconciliation: Ran queries to compare source and target data, investigated discrepancies, and helped validate report accuracy.

4. Financial and reporting integrity

A key consideration was ensuring that migration preserved important business values, not just the underlying records.

Financial consistency

Helped validate that financial balances in the legacy system were consistent with the corresponding results after migration.

Reporting accuracy

Used queries and product knowledge to compare source and target data, investigate differences, and check whether reports reflected the expected results.

5. Validation and automation

Initial validation involved working with sample data and comparing source and target results. Queries and troubleshooting helped identify discrepancies that needed further investigation.

The engineering team subsequently automated validation steps. The current ETL solution also generates reports highlighting discrepancies affecting reporting, which support ongoing bug investigation and resolution.

I remain loosely involved in troubleshooting when needed. The validation capability has therefore become part of the ongoing process for identifying data and reporting issues, rather than being limited to the initial migration.

6. Delivery approach

  • Gather requirements and clarify migration scope with the client.
  • Review sample data and define field mappings.
  • Write user stories and coordinate requirements with developers.
  • Validate migrated data against expected results.
  • Investigate financial and reporting discrepancies.
  • Support the evolution of validation into an automated process.
  • Use discrepancy reports to support ongoing product quality improvements.

7. Outcome and ongoing impact

  • Delivered an ETL capability that accepts client migration files and loads transformed data into the target system.
  • Established validation practices for checking migrated data and investigating discrepancies.
  • Contributed to automated validation and discrepancy reporting that supports ongoing issue investigation.
  • Helped address the need for financial consistency and accurate reporting during data migration.

No numerical improvement is claimed here because verified measurements have not yet been established for publication.

8. What this project demonstrates

  • Enterprise client requirement gathering.
  • Data analysis and field mapping.
  • User story creation and requirements translation.
  • Cross-functional coordination with engineering.
  • SQL-based validation and discrepancy investigation.
  • Financial and reporting reconciliation.
  • Continuous improvement through validation automation.

9. My approach

My focus was on connecting the client's business requirements with the engineering team's implementation. This involved understanding the source data, documenting the required transformations, validating the resulting data, and helping ensure that financial and reporting expectations were met.

The project reflects how I approach product delivery: clarify the business need, translate it into actionable requirements, work closely with technical teams, validate outcomes, and use identified issues to improve the product.
