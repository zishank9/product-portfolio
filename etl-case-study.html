1. The Problem

An enterprise hospitality client needed to migrate historical hotel and guest data from a legacy Property Management System (PMS) into the HotelKey platform.

The source data was provided in large, compressed XML files. The information needed to be analysed, mapped to the target system's data structure, transformed, and loaded into the new platform.

A successful migration required more than transferring records. Financial balances needed to remain consistent, and reports needed to reflect the migrated data correctly.

The central challenge: Data needed to be transferred into the new platform while preserving the business meaning of the original records, especially for financial calculations and reporting.

2. The Objective

Help define and deliver a repeatable ETL process that could migrate the required client data while maintaining data integrity and improving confidence in financial and reporting results.

  • Understand and document client migration requirements.
  • Map source data fields to the target data structure.
  • Translate requirements into actionable user stories.
  • Support engineering in building the ETL solution.
  • Validate migrated data against expected results.
  • Identify discrepancies affecting financial balances and reports.

5. Protecting Financial and Reporting Integrity

An important part of the project was checking that migrated information remained meaningful to the business. Data could be present in the new system but still produce incorrect financial or reporting results.

FOCUS 01

Financial Consistency

Helped validate that financial balances in the legacy system were consistent with corresponding results after migration.

FOCUS 02

Reporting Accuracy

Used queries and product knowledge to compare source and target data, investigate differences, and check whether reports reflected expected results.

Key consideration: Successful migration was not just about moving data. The migrated information also needed to support expected business calculations, financial consistency, and reporting.

6. From Manual Validation to Automation

Initial validation involved analysing sample data and comparing source and target results. SQL queries and troubleshooting helped identify discrepancies that required further investigation.

The engineering team subsequently automated validation steps. The ETL solution also generates discrepancy reports that help identify reporting differences and support bug investigation.

I remain loosely involved in troubleshooting when needed. Validation therefore continues to support ongoing data and reporting issue investigation beyond the initial migration work.
