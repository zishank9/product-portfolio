1. Project Background

During my work on enterprise hospitality software, I was involved in taking product and client requirements through development, validation, and delivery. These requirements could involve improving existing functionality, addressing business needs, or introducing behaviour required by an enterprise client.

Each requirement needed to be understood in its business context and translated into work that developers could implement and QA could validate. The delivery process also required coordination when questions, dependencies, or unexpected behaviour arose.

My responsibility was to connect the original business expectation with the work being performed by technical teams, helping maintain alignment from initial clarification through delivery and post-delivery support.

2. The Problem

A high-level feature request does not always explain every detail needed for implementation. Business stakeholders may describe the desired outcome without specifying all the functional rules, exceptions, dependencies, or validation scenarios.

If those details remain unclear, different teams can develop different interpretations of the same requirement. This can lead to additional clarification, unexpected behaviour during testing, or gaps between what was requested and what was delivered.

The challenge was to create a shared understanding of the requirement and maintain that understanding while the feature moved through development, testing, and delivery.

Core challenge: Translate business expectations into clear, actionable requirements while keeping engineering, QA, and delivery stakeholders aligned throughout the feature lifecycle.

3. The Objective

Support the delivery of product features that met the documented business requirements and were appropriately validated before being delivered to clients.

  • Understand the business problem and intended outcome.
  • Gather missing information and clarify functional requirements.
  • Translate requirements into user stories and epics where appropriate.
  • Communicate expected behaviour to development teams.
  • Coordinate dependencies and follow up on outstanding questions.
  • Perform product-level testing and support QA validation.
  • Help investigate discrepancies and track issue resolution.
  • Support client delivery, go-live activities, and post-delivery troubleshooting where required.

6. Translating Requirements into Actionable Work

Requirements needed to provide enough context for the development team to understand what was expected and for QA to determine how the functionality should be validated. I worked to clarify the business need before and during implementation.

Key areas of clarification

  • Business objective: What problem does the requested feature solve?
  • Expected behaviour: What should happen when the user or system performs a particular action?
  • Business rules: What conditions or restrictions influence the outcome?
  • Dependencies: Are other systems, configurations, or teams involved?
  • Exceptions: How should missing, invalid, or unexpected information be handled?
  • Validation: What would demonstrate that the requirement had been met?

Clarifying these areas helped create a more consistent understanding of the feature and provided a useful reference during development and testing.

7. User Stories and Acceptance Criteria

I used user stories and supporting documentation to translate business needs into actionable development work. The level of detail depended on the feature and the information required by the team.

A useful user story communicates who needs the functionality, what they need, and why it matters. Acceptance criteria provide additional detail about the expected behaviour.

Illustrative example — not an actual client requirement

Example User Story

As an authorised user, I want the system to validate the information I submit so that I can correct invalid information before proceeding.

Example Acceptance Criteria

  • The system checks required information before accepting a submission.
  • Missing or invalid information is handled according to the agreed rules.
  • The user receives a clear indication when the submission cannot proceed.
  • A valid submission follows the expected workflow.
  • The resulting behaviour can be verified in the test environment.

The example illustrates how a broad request can be structured into verifiable expectations. Actual stories and acceptance criteria must reflect the relevant feature and agreed business requirements.

8. Collaboration with Development Teams

Once requirements were documented, I continued working with developers to clarify functional questions and help maintain alignment between the requested outcome and implementation. This was important when questions arose about existing functionality, business rules, or dependencies.

  • Explained the business context behind requested functionality.
  • Clarified requirements when developers identified gaps or ambiguities.
  • Discussed expected outcomes and relevant exception scenarios.
  • Followed up on outstanding questions and delivery dependencies.
  • Coordinated with other teams when issues crossed functional boundaries.
  • Raised discrepancies between the expected and observed behaviour.
My approach: Effective collaboration requires understanding the business requirement and the technical context well enough to ask useful questions, communicate clearly, and help the right people resolve issues.

9. Product Testing and QA Collaboration

I performed product-level testing before QA handoff to check whether implemented functionality matched the documented requirements. I also supported QA with business context and testing setup when needed.

Areas of validation

  • Compare the implemented behaviour with the original requirement.
  • Check the expected user flow and relevant business rules.
  • Consider applicable error and exception scenarios.
  • Identify unexpected behaviour and document findings.
  • Work with developers to clarify discrepancies and investigate defects.
  • Help QA understand the expected outcome when validating a feature.

These checks complemented the QA process rather than replacing it. The objective was to identify mismatches early and ensure that testing remained connected to the intended business outcome.

10. Issue Investigation and Resolution

When unexpected behaviour appeared during testing or delivery, I helped clarify the difference between the expected result and what the system actually did. This provided a starting point for investigation and coordination with the relevant technical team.

Understand the Issue

Clarify the reported behaviour, expected result, relevant context, and steps needed to reproduce it where possible.

Coordinate Investigation

Share relevant details with developers or QA and help establish the next steps for investigation.

Validate the Outcome

Check the behaviour after a proposed fix or clarification and help confirm whether the original concern has been addressed.

This approach helped keep issues connected to the original requirement instead of treating them as isolated technical tasks without business context.

11. Delivery Readiness and Client Support

Feature delivery required coordination beyond implementation. The relevant teams needed visibility into testing status, unresolved issues, dependencies, and the work remaining before or after client handoff.

  • Followed up on implementation and validation progress.
  • Helped identify outstanding clarifications and delivery concerns.
  • Supported communication between teams involved in delivery.
  • Helped assess whether the feature matched the agreed requirement.
  • Supported client delivery and go-live activities where required.
  • Helped investigate post-delivery issues and clarify expected behaviour.
Delivery principle: Make it clear what has been completed, what has been validated, which issues remain open, and what needs attention during handoff or follow-up.

16. My Approach to Product Delivery

This experience strengthened my ability to work across business and technical teams and to understand how requirements influence implementation, testing, and delivery. It reinforced the importance of asking clarifying questions, documenting expected behaviour, and maintaining communication as work progresses.

I learned that successful product delivery is not simply about tracking tasks to completion. It also requires understanding why a feature is needed, translating that need into actionable requirements, helping teams resolve ambiguity, and validating that the final behaviour meets the intended objective.

My delivery philosophy: Understand the business need, define the expected outcome, align the teams doing the work, validate the result, and follow through on delivery and support.
