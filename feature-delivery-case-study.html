1. Overview

Enterprise hospitality software supports everyday hotel operations, where a feature change can affect staff workflows, business rules, and connected systems. My work involved translating business requests into actionable requirements and staying involved through development, validation, and client delivery.

2. The Challenge

A stakeholder may know the result they want without specifying permissions, exceptions, dependencies, or what QA should verify. Without that detail, business, engineering, and QA can interpret the same request differently. The challenge was to create shared understanding early and maintain it through delivery.

The key challenge: Keep the business outcome, implementation details, and testing expectations aligned as the request evolves.

3. My Objective

Help teams deliver functionality that reflects the agreed business need: clarify the problem, define testable expectations, coordinate functional questions, validate behaviour, and support a well-informed handoff.

  • Understand the problem and expected outcome.
  • Make requirements actionable and testable.
  • Support functional decisions across teams.
  • Validate results and follow through on delivery.

6. Clarifying Requirements

I explored the intended outcome, who would use the feature, relevant business rules, dependencies, exceptions, and what successful validation would look like. These discussions informed the user stories and questions shared with engineering and QA.

Illustrative example · Hotel check-in

From a request to clear requirements

Suppose a hotel wants the system to flag missing mandatory guest information before check-in. I would clarify which fields are required, whether rules vary by reservation type, what staff should see, and whether authorised exceptions are allowed.

Those decisions would become documented business rules and test scenarios—not assumptions left for development or QA to interpret.

7. User Stories & Acceptance Criteria

I translated clarified needs into user stories and supporting acceptance criteria, making expected behaviour easier to discuss, implement, and test.

Illustrative example · Hotel check-in

Example user story

As a front desk associate, I want to know when mandatory guest information is missing so that I can correct it before completing check-in.

Example acceptance criteria

  • Given all mandatory information is present, when check-in is submitted, then the normal workflow can proceed.
  • Given required information is missing, when check-in is attempted, then the system identifies the missing information.
  • The system follows the agreed rules for blocking check-in or permitting an authorised exception.
  • After the information is corrected, the associate can retry the workflow.

8. Working With Engineering

I explained the business context behind requests, clarified functional questions, followed up on dependencies, and coordinated with other teams when issues crossed system boundaries. My role was to help resolve ambiguity, not to write application code.

In practice: When a developer asked how a particular exception should behave, I helped clarify the business rule with the relevant stakeholder and brought the decision back to the team.

9. Product Validation & QA

Before QA handoff, I checked implemented behaviour against requirements and relevant user flows. I shared findings with developers and supported QA with business context, expected results, and investigation of discrepancies.

  • Compare the implemented flow with the requirement.
  • Check relevant business rules and error scenarios.
  • Record unexpected results with enough context for investigation.
  • Help QA confirm expected outcomes.

10. Investigating Issues

When observed behaviour differed from expectations, I helped establish the steps, context, and expected result; worked with engineering or QA on investigation; and checked whether subsequent changes addressed the original concern.

Describe

Capture what happened, what should have happened, and how to reproduce it.

Coordinate

Share the context with the right engineering or QA team and track open questions.

Verify

Check whether the agreed behaviour is restored after investigation or a fix.

11. Delivery & Follow-through

Delivery required visibility into validation status, outstanding issues, dependencies, and client readiness. I supported handoff and go-live activities where needed, and helped coordinate post-delivery troubleshooting.

  • Review validation progress and unresolved concerns.
  • Keep relevant stakeholders informed about dependencies.
  • Support client handoff and go-live when required.
  • Coordinate investigation of issues reported after delivery.

16. What I Learned

Feature delivery is more than moving tickets to completion. It requires understanding why a change matters, translating it into testable expectations, helping teams resolve ambiguity, and following through when the feature reaches users.

My approach: Understand the need, define the expected behaviour, connect the right people, validate the result, and follow through.
