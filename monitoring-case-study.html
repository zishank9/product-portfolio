Case Study 02 · Cloud Operations

Building a More Consistent Cloud Monitoring Process

How I helped establish monitoring practices, coordinate a 15-person team, and improve incident investigation through clearer dashboards, reusable documentation, and more consistent alert handling.

Role

Product Delivery Manager / Team Lead

Team

15-person monitoring team

Tools

Splunk, Datadog, AWS CloudWatch

Focus

Monitoring, incidents, and process improvement

The Challenge

Application monitoring had several operational challenges. Excessive or repetitive alerts made it harder to identify important issues, some problems were missed during testing and discovered later, and troubleshooting practices were not sufficiently consistent across the team.

The team needed a more structured way to review application health, investigate recurring failures, document known issues, and coordinate with developers and QA.

My Objective

My objective was to establish a more consistent monitoring and incident-handling process, improve visibility into recurring application problems, and help the team identify and investigate issues more effectively.

Core principle: Make monitoring actionable. The goal was not simply to collect more alerts, but to help the team distinguish meaningful signals from noise and respond consistently.

My Role and Contributions

1. Monitoring dashboards and alert quality

  • Created monitoring dashboards using the team's observability tools to improve visibility into application behaviour and recurring issues.
  • Reviewed repetitive alerts and worked on reducing unnecessary monitoring noise.
  • Helped distinguish higher-priority application problems from lower-priority warnings.
  • Worked on alert thresholds and filtering practices to make monitoring information more actionable.

2. Standard operating procedures and troubleshooting

  • Created SOPs and troubleshooting guides for common application failures.
  • Documented repeatable investigation steps so that the team could approach known issues more consistently.
  • Improved knowledge sharing and made it easier for team members to learn established troubleshooting practices.

3. Team leadership and coordination

  • Led and coordinated a 15-person cloud monitoring team.
  • Established more consistent approaches to alert handling, incident investigation, and escalation.
  • Coordinated with developers and QA to investigate application errors, follow up on bugs, and improve issue visibility.
  • Tracked operational performance and areas where monitoring and investigation processes could improve.

My Approach

  1. Identify patterns: Review recurring alerts and application failures to understand where noise or repeated investigation was affecting the team.
  2. Improve visibility: Use monitoring dashboards and alert refinement to make relevant application problems easier to identify.
  3. Standardize investigation: Document common troubleshooting steps and establish clearer alert-handling and escalation practices.
  4. Collaborate across teams: Coordinate with developers and QA when investigating bugs or issues requiring further technical analysis.
  5. Review and improve: Track recurring issues and operational observations to identify opportunities for further improvement.

Outcomes and Impact

The work helped establish a more structured and repeatable approach to cloud monitoring and incident investigation. The key observed outcomes included:

  • Reduced application monitoring noise.
  • More consistent troubleshooting practices.
  • Better visibility into recurring application issues.
  • Improved identification of application problems.
  • Better coordination between monitoring, developers, and QA.
  • More opportunities to identify issues before clients reported them.
  • Easier onboarding and knowledge transfer through reusable SOPs and troubleshooting guides.

These outcomes are described qualitatively because I do not have verified numerical measurements to publish.

Key Learnings

  • Effective monitoring depends on signal quality, not simply the volume of alerts collected.
  • Clear documentation makes operational knowledge reusable and reduces reliance on individual team members.
  • Incident management works best when monitoring, development, and QA collaborate around shared evidence and clear ownership.
  • Sustainable operational improvements require consistent processes, team coordination, and ongoing review.
